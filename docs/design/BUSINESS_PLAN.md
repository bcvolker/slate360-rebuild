# Slate360 LLC — Business Plan (draft v2, 2026-08-22)

**Model:** Brian performs on-site capture. Slate360 produces digital twins and documentation
for contractors. Sold as **individual contracts on purchase orders** — priced per project by
number of scans, frequency, and how long the client keeps platform access. **No SaaS
subscriptions.**

Numbers marked **measured** come from real jobs on this pipeline. Everything about pricing,
throughput and market response is *assumed* until a customer proves it.

---

## 0. The constraint that shapes everything

**Brian has a job.** This is a side business until it replaces that income. Every decision
below is filtered through: *does this work in evenings and weekends, and does it build toward
the point where quitting is safe?*

That has three consequences worth stating up front:

1. **Cash flow matters more than scale** in phase one. A $2,000 website job next month beats a
   $20,000 institutional contract in eighteen months.
2. **Capacity is severely limited** — realistically 2–6 scan days a month, not 15–20.
3. **Anything with a long lead time should start NOW, in parallel**, because the waiting
   happens whether or not he is available to work. See §7 on ASU.

## 1. The website currently sells a different business

`slate360.ai` today sells self-serve app subscriptions: Site Walk Standard at $787/year, Pro
at $1,484, a bundle at $3,476, free trials, credit packs, app-store links. Headlines are
*"Capture the site. Keep the twin."* and *"Walk the job. It documents itself."*

That is a **product-led SaaS** pitch. The actual business is a **done-for-you service** sold
on purchase orders. Those are different buyers, different price anchors, different sales
motions — and a contractor who lands on that page concludes he is being asked to buy an app
and do the work himself.

**This is the highest-leverage non-technical fix available.** The site should lead with the
service. Suggested reframing:

| now | should be |
|---|---|
| "Start free trial" | "Request a site visit" |
| App tiers and pricing | Example projects and deliverables |
| "It documents itself" | "We document it for you" |
| App store badges | Case study, sample twin, contact |

The apps stay — as **how the work gets delivered**, not as what is being sold. If app sales
ever become a line, it is a later decision, not the current shop window.

## 2. Two revenue lines

### Line A — Capture and digital twins (the main business)

A contractor buys a documented, measurable, walkable record of their job. Sold per project on
a PO.

### Line B — Digital services for small contractors (the near-term cash line)

Website upgrades, 360 tours, digital presence work for small contractors who lack the staff or
the hours to do it themselves.

This line is not a distraction, for three reasons:
- **It pays now**, at a point when capture volume is low and time is scarce.
- **It uses the same skills and the same tooling** already built.
- **It is a warm-lead machine.** A contractor who trusts Slate360 with their website is a
  qualified prospect for scanning, and the conversation happens naturally: *"while I was
  shooting your 360 tour, here is what we could do on an active job."*

Treat Line B as the **wedge and the runway**, Line A as the destination.

| Progression history, date comparison | Disputes, claims, "was that there in March?" |
| Client portal + controlled reshare | They look sophisticated to *their* client |

All of the above except 360 fusion is built and validated on a real capture today.

**Thermal:** Brian can perform thermal analysis and it remains a genuine add-on capability.
His ITC Level III certification is **expired and will not be renewed**, so **it must never be
claimed** — not on the site, not in a proposal, not in a report. Sell the *finding*
("moisture intrusion documented at this location"), never a credential. Where a client needs
certified thermography for an insurance or legal purpose, refer it out.

## 4. Pricing — contracts, not subscriptions

Every engagement is a quoted project on a PO. Three variables set the number:

1. **Scope per visit** — area, complexity, how much must be captured before it is covered.
2. **Frequency** — one visit, or a schedule across the job.
3. **Access duration** — how long the client keeps the portal live.

*All figures assumed and need validation on the first paid job.*

| | *assumed* |
|---|---|
| Single visit, one area or small suite | $400–800 |
| Single visit, floor / ~2,000 sq ft | $900–1,500 |
| Multi-floor or full building | $2,000–4,000 |
| Scheduled progression, per visit within a contract | $600–1,000 |
| Platform access, 12 months, bundled into the contract | $600–1,200 |
| Access renewal beyond contract term | $300–600/year |

Structuring access as a **contract line item with a term** rather than a subscription is
exactly right for PO-driven buyers — it fits how a GC or an institution actually procures, and
it avoids the recurring-billing relationship they do not want.

**Line B pricing** *(assumed)*: website refresh $1,500–4,000; 360 tour $500–1,500; combined
package $2,500–6,000.

## 5. Unit economics — **measured**

| item | cost |
|---|---|
| Mesh + dollhouse + floor plan + take-off | **$0.05–0.15** (CPU, 3–6 min) |
| Photoreal walkthrough, one room | **$0.60–2.50** (GPU, 19–68 min) |
| Large multi-zone site | $2–8 |
| Storage, per project per year | single-digit dollars |

**Compute is under 1% of revenue.** A $1,200 project costs a few dollars to process.

Strategic consequence: **there is no cost reason to limit re-processing, re-scanning, or
keeping history.** Long access terms are nearly pure margin, and generosity there is a real
competitive weapon against vendors protecting a per-scan cost structure.

## 6. Revenue path, phased around the day job

*Illustrative, assumed.*

**Phase 1 — Prove it (months 1–4).** Network-led. Two or three Line B jobs for cash, and
**one paid capture project at real price**. Target $2–5k/month combined. Goal is not revenue;
it is a reference, a case study, and a real price point.

**Phase 2 — Build the base (months 4–10).** Five to eight repeat contractor relationships,
progression work on at least one active job. Target $6–10k/month. **This is where the
quit-decision data comes from.**

**Phase 3 — Replace income (months 10–18).** Consistent $10–15k/month across both lines, with
enough forward-booked work to cover 3–6 months of expenses. **Then, and only then, quit.**

**Phase 4 — Institutional.** ASU and similar. See below.

## 7. ASU Capital Programs Management — the strategic asset

Brian knows how CPMG runs projects from the inside, and **Multivista is the named incumbent**.
That is a genuine, specific, defensible advantage: he knows what they actually need, what they
currently overpay for, and how their procurement works.

**One important correction to sequencing.** The plan was: quit the job, then pursue ASU.
That is likely backwards.

**Getting qualified is paperwork, not availability.** ARFQ listing, vendor registration,
insurance certificates, W-9, references — that is a months-long queue that runs whether or not
Brian is free to work. **Start it now, while employed.** The *work* requires availability; the
*qualification* does not. Being on the list when he quits is worth far more than starting the
list after.

Practical steps, in parallel with everything else:
1. Confirm the current ARFQ cycle and submission window.
2. Verify insurance requirements — public institutions usually specify GL and professional
   limits well above what a small LLC carries by default. **Price this in; it is a real cost.**
3. Assemble qualifications: entity docs, references, sample deliverable, capability statement.
4. Build a one-page comparison against the incumbent's model — not on price alone, but on
   *what the deliverable does that theirs does not* (measurable twin, plenum view, take-off).

**Two things to check rather than assume:**
- **Conflict of interest / post-employment rules.** If Brian's current or recent employment
  touches ASU or its contractors, there may be procurement restrictions on bidding. This is a
  question for the institution's procurement office or a lawyer — worth five minutes now
  rather than discovering it inside a submission.
- **Public procurement is slow.** Months to get listed, more before first work. It cannot be
  the plan for replacing income; it is the plan for what comes after.

## 8. Go-to-market — network first

Brian's local construction network is the entire phase-one channel, and it is a good one.

**The approach that works with contractors:** not a pitch. Show them a twin of a job they
know, on a phone, in ninety seconds. The product demonstrates itself; a deck does not.

Sequence:
1. **Build one showcase.** A real, complete, well-executed twin — ideally a space a local
   contact recognises. This is the entire sales asset.
2. **Work the network directly.** Coffee, job sites, trade association meetings. Ask what they
   currently do about documentation and what it costs them when it goes wrong.
3. **Lead with the pre-cover story**, not "3D tour." *"Scan it before you cover it"* is a
   different budget line and a different urgency — one is marketing, the other is risk.
4. **Take the first job cheap if needed** for a reference and a case study. Once.
5. **Line B as the door-opener** where capture is not an immediate fit.

**What not to do:** paid ads, cold outreach, or a self-serve funnel. Wrong channel for this
buyer at this stage, and the current site is built for exactly that.

## 9. Competitive position

| | Matterport | Multivista | **Slate360** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walkable twin | strong | weak | strong |
| Measurement / take-off | weak | none | **net of openings, exportable** |
| Progression over time | weak | **strong** | **strong** |
| Document pinning | weak | strong | **survives re-scan** |
| Plenum / above-ceiling | **absent** | photos only | **present** |
| Interior + exterior, one site | weak | weak | designed for it |
| Accuracy honesty | implies precision | n/a | **labelled per surface** |
| Cost structure | high | high | **compute under 1% of revenue** |

The gap nobody occupies: **an evidentiary, measurable, time-aware twin with a filing
cabinet.** Matterport has the walk without the evidence. Multivista has the evidence without
the walk or the measurement.

**The durable advantages are two, and neither is a credential:**
- **Cost structure.** Delivering what a $50k rig delivers, at compute costs under 1% of
  revenue, means underbidding an incumbent while making better margin.
- **Accuracy honesty.** Every measurement labelled verified or estimated, every unscanned
  surface visibly grey. Competitors imply precision they cannot support. With professionals,
  being the one who states it plainly is a position that holds.

## 10. Positioning and the method

Client-facing material describes **outcomes and accuracy, never equipment**. No device names
in any deliverable, share page, PDF, or on the site. The QC card states what the accuracy *is*
— verified/estimated, the residual, that a laser governs. It never states what produced it.

This is commercial protection *and* good practice: the number is the claim, the kit is an
implementation detail. It also keeps the door open — if the pipeline later runs on different
sensors, nothing client-facing changes.

## 11. Risks

| risk | severity | mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Liability from a bad measurement** | **highest** | Estimating-grade language, per-surface labelling, laser governs, never an EoR claim. Carry insurance. |
| **Claiming an expired credential** | **high** | Thermal sold as a finding, never as certification. Refer out anything requiring certified thermography. |
| Website sells the wrong business | high | §1 — reframe to service-led |
| Time — the day job | high | Phase around it; do not overcommit and miss a deadline |
| Solo dependency | high | SOP is written; train an operator once volume justifies it |
| ASU timeline slips | medium | Never the income-replacement plan; start qualification early |
| Price resistance | medium | Lead with pre-cover evidence, not "3D tour" |
| 360 fusion never fully automates | **low** | Already demoted — the twin ships without it |

## 13. What is unknown

Stated plainly rather than assumed away:

1. **Price tolerance.** Every figure in §4 is a guess until a contractor pays one.
2. **Whether clients value the platform or just want a link.** This decides whether access
   terms are a real revenue line or a giveaway. **The first three customers answer it.**
3. **Sales cycle length** in this network.
4. **Repeat rate on progression work** — the model leans on it and it is unproven.
5. **True capacity** around a full-time job.
6. **ASU procurement specifics** — cycle, insurance thresholds, conflict rules.
7. **Whether Line B competes with Line A for the same scarce hours.** It may. Watch it.
