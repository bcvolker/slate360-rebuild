# Slate360 LLC — Business Plan

*Draft v3 · 2026-08-22 · Written after the pivot from software subscriptions to services*

---

## 1. Executive summary

**Slate360 LLC is a reality-capture and technical services business for the building
industry.** Brian visits sites, captures them, and delivers documentation his clients cannot
produce themselves — digital twins, 360 tours, thermal surveys — plus technical builds
(websites, dashboards, project tooling) for small organisations trying to modernise.

**Sold as services on individual contracts and purchase orders. Not software subscriptions.**

The business rests on an unusual cost position: proprietary processing built in-house turns
inexpensive field equipment into deliverables that normally require $50,000 hardware and a
crew. Compute costs run **under 1% of revenue** — measured, not estimated. That allows
underbidding established vendors while holding better margin, and permits generosity
(unlimited re-processing, long archive access) that competitors protecting hardware and
headcount cannot match.

**Current status:** the capture and processing pipeline is built and validated on real
captures. The pivot is recent; the website, the operator dashboard, and the client portal all
need to be brought in line with the service model.

**Constraint:** Brian is employed full-time. This is a side business until it replaces that
income. Every phase below is built around that.

## 2. What the business does

Four service lines, sharing one set of skills, one toolchain, and one buyer network.

### Line 1 — Digital twins
Measurable, walkable 3D records of a space. Floor plans, area take-offs, document pinning,
progression over time, a portal the client shares with their own clients.

*Buyers:* general contractors, construction managers, owners, facility managers.
*Trigger:* something is about to be covered up, or nobody has reliable as-builts.

### Line 2 — 360 tours
Navigable photographic tours. Faster and cheaper than a twin, no measurement claims.

*Buyers:* contractors, architects, real estate, small businesses.
*Trigger:* marketing, a record of a finished space, a walkthrough for a remote party.

### Line 3 — Thermography
Thermal survey and reporting: moisture intrusion, missing insulation, electrical and
mechanical anomalies. Pairs naturally with a twin — thermal images pinned in place.

*Buyers:* contractors, building owners, property managers.
*Important:* Brian's ITC Level III certification is **expired and will not be renewed**. Sell
the *finding*, documented and located, never the credential. Anything requiring certified
thermography for insurance or legal purposes is referred out. This is a firm rule.

### Line 4 — Technical builds
Websites, dashboards, project-management tooling for small firms without the staff or hours
to build them.

*Buyers:* small contractors, subs, specialty trades, small design firms.
*Trigger:* they are losing work to better-presented competitors.

**Why four lines and not one:** Line 4 pays now, while capture volume is still building. It
uses tooling that already exists. And it is a warm-lead machine — a contractor who trusts
Slate360 with their website is a qualified prospect for scanning, and the conversation happens
on site, naturally.

## 3. Market and buyers

Broader than contractors alone. Every organisation that touches a building needs a record of
it at some point.

| buyer | what they need | what they pay for |
|---|---|---|
| **General contractors** | Pre-cover documentation, progress records, dispute evidence | Risk reduction |
| **Architects** | Existing conditions before design | Not measuring a building by hand |
| **Engineers** | As-builts, above-ceiling conditions, MEP coordination | Accuracy before commitment |
| **Owners / facility managers** | Handover records, O&M, what is behind the wall | An asset that outlives the contractor |
| **Institutions** (universities, districts, municipalities) | Standardised documentation across a portfolio | Consistency and procurement compliance |
| **Small firms** (Line 4) | Digital presence and project tooling | Competitiveness |

**The strongest single trigger across all of them:** *something is about to become invisible.*
A ceiling closing, a wall going up, a slab pouring. That moment recurs on every job, every
floor, every phase — and it is the difference between a nice-to-have and evidence.

Lead with **"document it before you cover it."** That is a different budget line and a
different urgency than "3D tour."

## 4. Competitive position

| | Matterport | Multivista | Local surveyors | **Slate360** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Walkable record | strong | weak | none | strong |
| Measurement / take-off | weak | none | **survey-grade** | net of openings, exportable |
| Progression over time | weak | **strong** | none | strong |
| Document pinning | weak | strong | none | survives re-scan |
| Above-ceiling / plenum | absent | photos only | rarely | **present** |
| Thermal | absent | absent | absent | **available** |
| Technical builds | absent | absent | absent | **available** |
| Cost structure | high | high | very high | **compute <1% of revenue** |

**The gap nobody occupies:** an evidentiary, measurable, time-aware record with a filing
cabinet attached — delivered as a service, at a price a mid-size contractor approves without
a committee.

**Three durable advantages:**

1. **Cost structure.** Delivering what a $50k rig delivers at near-zero marginal processing
   cost means underbidding incumbents while making better margin.
2. **Breadth.** No competitor offers twins, tours, thermal *and* technical builds. For a small
   client, one trusted vendor beats four.
3. **Accuracy honesty.** Every measurement labelled verified or estimated; every unscanned
   surface visibly grey. Competitors imply precision they cannot support. With professionals,
   stating it plainly is a position that holds — and it is also the liability defence.

**Not a differentiator:** the equipment. Client-facing material describes outcomes and
accuracy, never devices. The number is the claim; the kit is an implementation detail.

## 5. Pricing

Every engagement is a quoted project on a contract or PO. Three variables set the number:
**scope per visit**, **frequency**, and **how long the client keeps access**.

*All figures assumed and require validation on the first paid jobs.*

### Capture (Lines 1–3)

| | *assumed* |
|---|---|
| Single visit — one area / small suite | $400–800 |
| Single visit — floor, ~2,000 sq ft | $900–1,500 |
| Multi-floor or full building | $2,000–4,000 |
| Scheduled progression, per visit within a contract | $600–1,000 |
| 360 tour only | $500–1,500 |
| Thermal survey add-on | $400–900 |
| Platform access, 12 months, bundled into the contract | $600–1,200 |
| Access renewal beyond term | $300–600/year |

### Technical builds (Line 4)

| | *assumed* |
|---|---|
| Website refresh | $1,500–4,000 |
| Dashboard / project tooling | $3,000–10,000 |
| Combined package with a tour | $2,500–6,000 |

**Structure access as a contract line item with a term**, never a subscription. That is how
PO-driven buyers procure, and it avoids the recurring-billing relationship institutional
clients specifically do not want.

## 6. Unit economics — **measured**

| | cost |
|---|---|
| Mesh, dollhouse, floor plan, take-off | **$0.05–0.15** (CPU, 3–6 min) |
| Photoreal walkthrough, one room | **$0.60–2.50** (GPU, 19–68 min) |
| Large multi-zone site | $2–8 |
| Storage, per project per year | single digit dollars |

**Compute is under 1% of revenue.** A $1,200 project costs a few dollars to process.

Two consequences worth designing around:
- **Long access terms are nearly pure margin.** Offer them generously; it is a weapon.
- **Re-processing is free.** A scan can be re-run with better settings forever, at no cost. No
  competitor with hardware and crews can say that.

### Capacity — the real constraint

Time, not money.

| | *assumed* |

| Processing | unattended |
| QC and publish | 30–60 min |
| **Available scan days while employed** | **2–6/month** |
| **Projects/month while employed** | **2–5** |

## 7. Operations

### How a job runs

1. **Scope and quote.** Site details, what is being covered and when, access duration.
2. **Capture.** LiDAR walk for geometry, 360 at two heights for detail, thermal if contracted.
   Per the written capture SOP.
3. **Upload.** Phone capture plus 360 and drone files from the desk.
4. **Process.** Unattended. Measurable deliverable in minutes; photoreal overnight.
5. **QC.** Coverage ratio, storey height against a known standard, fusion residual, untextured
   fraction. **A failing scan is re-shot, never shipped.**
6. **Publish.** Into the client's portal. They compose a controlled share for their own client.
7. **Invoice** against the PO.

### What still has to be built

Three pieces of infrastructure, in priority order:

**a. The website must sell the service.** It currently sells app subscriptions — free trials,
annual tiers, app-store badges. That is a product-led SaaS pitch for a done-for-you services
business, and a contractor landing there concludes he is being asked to buy an app and do the
work himself. **This is the highest-leverage non-technical fix available.**

| now | should be |
|---|---|
| "Start free trial" | "Request a site visit" |
| App tiers and pricing | Example projects and deliverables |
| "It documents itself" | "We document it for you" |
| App store badges | Case study, sample twin, contact |

**b. An operator dashboard.** The console Brian works from: projects, captures, processing
status, QC results, deliverable assembly, publishing, and client access management — across
all four service lines. This is the difference between a business and a series of heroic
efforts. It does not exist yet and is the main internal build.

**c. The client portal.** Where deliverables live and clients reshare them. Substantially
built; needs the share-package model finished.

## 8. Go-to-market

**Phase one is entirely network-led.** Brian's existing local construction relationships are
the channel.

**What works with this buyer:** not a deck. Show them a twin of a building they know, on a
phone, in ninety seconds. The product demonstrates itself.

1. **Build one showcase** — a real, complete, well-executed twin of a recognisable local
   space. This is the entire sales asset.
2. **Work the network directly.** Coffee, job sites, association meetings. Ask what they do
   about documentation today and what it costs when it goes wrong.
3. **Lead with the pre-cover story.**
4. **Take the first job cheap** for a reference and a case study. Once.
5. **Use Line 4 as the door-opener** where capture is not an immediate fit.

**Do not** run paid ads, cold outreach, or a self-serve funnel. Wrong channel for this buyer —
and the current website is built for exactly that.

### Institutional: ASU Capital Programs Management

Brian knows how CPMG runs projects from the inside, and **Multivista is the named incumbent**.
He knows what they need, what they overpay for, and how their procurement works. That is the
most defensible commercial asset in this plan.

**Start the qualification now, while employed.** ARFQ listing, vendor registration, insurance
certificates, references — that is a months-long queue that runs on its own clock regardless
of availability. The *work* requires free time; the *paperwork* does not. Being on the list at
the moment of quitting is worth far more than starting the list that day.

Steps to run in parallel with everything else:
1. Confirm the current ARFQ cycle and submission window.
2. **Verify insurance requirements** — institutions typically specify general and professional
   liability well above what a small LLC carries by default. Price it in; it is real cost.
3. Assemble qualifications: entity docs, references, sample deliverable, capability statement.
4. Build a one-page comparison against the incumbent — not on price alone, but on what the
   deliverable does that theirs does not.

**Two things to verify rather than assume:**
- **Post-employment conflict rules.** If Brian's current or recent employment touches ASU or
  its contractors, restrictions on bidding may apply. A question for their procurement office
  or a lawyer — cheap now, expensive mid-submission.
- **Timeline.** Public procurement is slow. This is the plan for *after* income replacement,
  not the plan *for* it.

## 9. Financial plan

*Illustrative and assumed. The purpose is shape and sequencing, not forecast accuracy.*

**Phase 1 — Prove it (months 1–4).** Two or three Line 4 jobs for cash, plus **one paid
capture at real price**. Target **$2–5k/month**. The goal is a reference, a case study, and a
validated price point — not revenue.

**Phase 2 — Build the base (months 4–10).** Five to eight repeat relationships; progression
work on at least one active job. Target **$6–10k/month**. This is where the quit-decision data
comes from.

**Phase 3 — Replace income (months 10–18).** Consistent **$10–15k/month** with enough
forward-booked work to cover 3–6 months of expenses. **Then quit — not before.**

**Phase 4 — Institutional and scale.** ASU and similar. Consider training a second operator:
the kit is inexpensive, the SOP is written, and processing is unattended, so a second pair of
hands scales capacity without scaling cost.

|---|---|
| Equipment (already owned) | — |
| Printed control markers, consumables | negligible |
| Compute and storage | <$50/month at early volume |
| Website and hosting | existing |
| **Insurance (GL + professional)** | **$1,500–4,000/year — verify, and higher for institutional work** |
| LLC, accounting, legal | $1,000–3,000/year |
| Vehicle and travel | variable |

**The business is not capital-constrained.** It is time-constrained.

## 10. Risks

| risk | severity | mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Liability from a bad measurement** | **highest** | Estimating-grade language, per-surface labelling, laser governs, never an Engineer-of-Record claim. Carry adequate insurance. |
| **Claiming an expired credential** | **high** | Thermal sold as documented findings only. Refer out certified work. Never appears on the site or in a proposal. |
| Website sells the wrong business | high | §7a — reframe to service-led |
| Time — the day job | high | Phase around it. Do not overcommit and miss a deadline. |
| Solo dependency | high | SOP written; train an operator once volume justifies it |
| Price resistance | medium | Lead with evidence and risk, not "3D tour" |
| ASU timeline slips | medium | Never the income-replacement plan |
| Line 4 competes for the same hours as Lines 1–3 | medium | Watch it. Cap technical-build work if it crowds out capture. |

## 12. Open questions

Stated plainly rather than assumed away. These are the things that would most change the plan:

1. **Price tolerance.** Every figure in §5 is a guess until someone pays it.
2. **Do clients value ongoing access, or do they just want a link?** This decides whether
   access terms are a real revenue line or a giveaway. **The first three clients answer it.**
3. **Repeat rate on progression work.** The model leans on it; it is unproven.
4. **Sales cycle length** in this network.
5. **True capacity** around a full-time job.
6. **Whether Line 4 is a wedge or a trap.** It pays now and opens doors — but it consumes the
   same scarce hours as capture, and it is a different business. Revisit at month 6.
7. **ASU specifics** — cycle timing, insurance thresholds, conflict rules.
