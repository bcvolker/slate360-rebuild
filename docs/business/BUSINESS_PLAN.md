## 1. Executive Summary

Slate360 LLC is a **reality-capture and technical services** business for the building industry. Brian visits sites, captures them with a lean, high-accuracy toolchain, and delivers documentation, models, and visual aids that clients cannot efficiently produce themselves — digital twins, 360 tours, thermal surveys, and supporting technical builds (websites, dashboards, project tooling).

**Core offer:** Done-for-you services sold on individual contracts and purchase orders. Clients receive the technological tools, measurable models, visual aids, portals, and share packages they need to manage, market, document, and defend their projects.

This is **not** a self-serve software subscription business. The platform exists to make the services repeatable, high-quality, and scalable for the operator (and later a second operator), and to give clients a professional portal experience that outlives any single capture.

**Unusual cost position:** Proprietary in-house processing turns inexpensive field equipment (iPhone LiDAR + 360 + thermal) into deliverables that normally require $50k+ hardware and a multi-person crew. Measured compute costs run under 1% of revenue. This enables underbidding established vendors while holding superior margin, unlimited re-processing, and long archive access terms that competitors protecting hardware and headcount cannot match.

**Current state (Aug 2026):**
- Capture + processing pipeline: built, validated on real captures, deployed, 201 tests passing. Accuracy is measurable and honest (mesh diagonal, storey height, fusion residual).
- Operator dashboard: does not exist (highest-priority internal build).
- Client portal: partially exists but fragmented across three share systems.
- Website: still sells the old SaaS app model — must be reframed immediately.
- Technical builds capability: already exists and can generate cash now.

**Constraint:** Full-time employment. Every phase is sequenced around limited scan days (2–6/month) and available evenings/weekends. The business is time-constrained, not capital-constrained.

**Current focus & main hurdles (Aug 2026):**
1. Completing / hardening the digital twin pipeline
2. Reframing the website to the services model
3. Building the operator dashboard (create and manage deliverables)
4. Building the unified client portal with full features

Services have not yet started. Brian will be the primary user of the Site Walk and Twin 360 apps for field capture. App Store / Play Store distribution (and potential later subscription sales through the stores) remains a secondary track. Current priority is services readiness.

**Unifying trigger across all service lines:** Something is about to become invisible. A ceiling closing, a wall going up, a slab pouring, above-ceiling work finishing. "Document it before you cover it" is a different budget line and a different urgency than "3D tour."

## 2. What the Business Does — Four Service Lines, One Network

All four lines share the same skills, the same toolchain, the same relationship network, and ultimately the same operator console and client portal.

### Line 1 — Digital Twins
Measurable, walkable 3D records of a space. Floor plans, area take-offs, document pinning that survives re-scans, progression over time, a portal the client can share with their own stakeholders.

**Buyers:** General contractors, construction managers, owners, facility managers, architects (existing conditions), engineers (as-builts / above-ceiling).
**Trigger:** Something is about to be covered, or reliable as-builts do not exist.
**Value:** Risk reduction, dispute evidence, handover asset that outlives the contractor.

### Line 2 — 360 Tours
Navigable photographic tours. Faster and cheaper than a twin; no measurement claims.

**Buyers:** Contractors, architects, real estate, small businesses, institutions needing remote walkthroughs.
**Trigger:** Marketing, record of finished space, remote stakeholder access, progress documentation.

### Line 3 — Thermography
Thermal survey and reporting: moisture intrusion, missing insulation, electrical/mechanical anomalies. Naturally pairs with a twin (thermal images pinned in place).

**Important rule (non-negotiable):** ITC Level III certification is expired and will not be renewed. Sell the *finding*, documented and located. Never the credential. Anything requiring certified thermography for insurance or legal purposes is referred out. This language never appears on the website or in proposals.

**Buyers:** Contractors, building owners, property managers, facility teams.

### Line 4 — Technical Builds
Websites, dashboards, project-management tooling, and light custom software for small firms that lack the staff or hours to build them.

**Buyers:** Small contractors, specialty trades, design firms.
**Trigger:** They are losing work to better-presented competitors.
**Strategic role:** Pays cash now while capture volume builds. Uses tooling that already exists. Acts as a warm-lead machine — a contractor who trusts Slate360 with their website is a qualified scanning prospect, and the conversation happens naturally on site.

**Review gate:** Month 6. Cap Line 4 hours if it begins to crowd out capture work. It is a wedge, not the long-term core.

**The gap nobody occupies:** An evidentiary, measurable, time-aware record with a filing cabinet attached — delivered as a service, at a price a mid-size contractor can approve without a committee.

**Three durable advantages (none of them equipment):**

1. **Cost structure** — Delivering what a $50k rig delivers at near-zero marginal processing cost means underbidding incumbents while making better margin. Re-processing is free forever. Long access terms are nearly pure margin.
2. **Breadth** — No competitor offers twins + tours + thermal + technical builds. For a small client, one trusted vendor beats four.
3. **Accuracy honesty** — Every measurement labelled verified or estimated; every unscanned surface visibly grey. This is both a commercial position and the liability defence. Competitors imply precision they cannot support.

Client-facing material describes outcomes and accuracy only. Never devices, brands, or hardware.

**Open question (critical):** Do clients value ongoing access, or do they just want a link? First three clients will answer this. It materially changes the financial shape.

## 7. Operations & Platform

### How a job runs
1. Scope & quote (site details, what is being covered and when, access duration).
2. Capture (LiDAR walk + 360 at two heights + thermal if contracted) per written SOP.
3. Upload (phone manifest + 360/drone files from desk).
4. Process (unattended).
5. QC (coverage ratio, storey height vs known standard, fusion residual, untextured fraction). Failing scans are re-shot, never shipped.
6. Publish into client portal. Client composes controlled share packages for their own stakeholders.
7. Invoice against the PO.

# Reviewer Addendum — 2026-08-23

*The plan above is adopted as canonical. These are the points needing correction or a flag
before being acted on.*

## 1. ASU is now a live conflict of interest, not a hypothetical

**§8 says to start ASU vendor qualification now, while employed. Do not act on that without
checking ASU policy and speaking to their ethics or general counsel office first.**

That recommendation originated in an earlier draft written *before* it was known that Brian is
employed **by ASU Capital Programs Management Group** — the exact unit he intends to sell to.
It was sound advice for an outside vendor approaching an institution. It is not sound for an
employee approaching their own employer.

What changes:

- Arizona has statutory conflict-of-interest rules for public employees regarding contracts
  with their own agency (A.R.S. § 38-501 et seq.), generally requiring disclosure of a
  substantial interest and recusal from related decisions. ASU will additionally have its own
  policy on outside employment and on doing business with the university.
- Registering a wholly-owned LLC as a vendor to one's own department, while employed there, is
  precisely the fact pattern those rules exist to govern.
- The exposure is not only losing the opportunity — depending on the rules, it can affect
  current employment. That is the income this plan depends on until Phase 3.

**Recommended sequence instead:**

1. **Before any ASU-directed activity**, read ASU's outside-employment and conflict-of-interest
   policy and speak to their ethics or general counsel office. This is a routine enquiry;
   many universities permit outside businesses with disclosure.
2. **Disclose the LLC** through whatever process ASU requires, whether or not ASU work is
   pursued. Owning an outside business in an adjacent field is usually itself disclosable.
3. **Treat ASU as strictly post-employment**, and confirm whether a cooling-off period applies
   before bidding.
4. Meanwhile, aim the same insight at **other institutional buyers** — school districts,
   municipalities, community colleges, other universities. The knowledge of how capital
   programs procure and manage transfers cleanly; the conflict does not.

The insider knowledge remains genuinely valuable. The *timing* does not survive the new fact.

## 2. Pipeline hardening is ranked first; it should not be

§7 lists "completing / hardening the digital twin pipeline" as hurdle #1. The measured state
does not support that ranking. The pipeline already produces a validated, metric, measurable
twin at roughly ten cents of compute, checkable against external references (storey height
1.4%, mesh diagonal 3%).

**Nothing remaining in the pipeline stands between today and a first paid job. The operator
dashboard does.** Recommended order:

1. Website reframe — small, highest leverage
2. Operator dashboard v1
3. Client portal unification
4. Pipeline hardening — continuous, not a gate

The standing risk on this project is that the pipeline keeps improving while nothing becomes
sellable. Every work session so far has improved the twin; none has made it deliverable.

## 3. Smaller corrections

- **"Spate360"** appears twice in the accompanying skill files — the monetization skill title
  and the PWA skill body. Should be **Slate360**.
- **Insurance** has a milestone checkbox but no cost line in §9. Budget $1,500–4,000/year for
