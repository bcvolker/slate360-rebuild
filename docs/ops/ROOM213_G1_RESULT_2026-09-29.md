# Room 213 — R213-G1 normals-only controlled run: result (2026-09-29)

## Classification: **G1_NO_MEANINGFUL_CHANGE**

Normal supervision at the code-default weight (0.01) produced a model that is visually indistinguishable from golden at
ordinary viewing scale, and differs at 1:1 by small, mixed amounts. The measured differences:
- small gains in floaters, needle splats and chair slats;
- small losses in carpet and baseboard fine detail and in window-recess energy;
- no change on the white wall;
- held-out metrics equal within noise.

**It is not a regression.** No smoothing, lost thin structure or wrong surfaces are visible.

**It is not a pass.** No architectural target improves visibly.

Per the brief, **Spark was not evaluated**; that step is reserved for a PASS.

Evidence is in `docs/ops/room213-g1-2026-09-29/`. Code is in `workers/modal/room213-g1/`. Every artefact is on volume
`slate360-recon-experiments:room213/2026-09-28/g1/`: dataset, raw maps, per-frame gate JSON, run directory with all
checkpoints and the G1 PLY, eval renders for both models, and the walkthrough mp4.

The golden baseline is untouched. The golden images, masks and sparse model are used through symlinks and a
sha-verified copy. The golden PLY sha `7e7b5d18…` was re-verified at evaluation.

## 7. Gaussian-distribution comparison (secondary question)

| | golden | G1 |
|---|---|---|
| scale max p50 / p90 | 0.0107 / 0.060 | 0.0120 / 0.067 |
| opacity p50 / p90 | 0.136 / 0.518 | 0.162 / 0.589 |
| splats inside room box | 551 k | 519 k |
| splat flatness near walls (p50) | 2.8–4.8 | 2.8–3.6 |

**Answer.** G1 does **not** reduce the bright-front / darker-behind wall layering. The opacity behind every wall and
the ceiling *increases* by 3–9 points, and large near-plane splats increase. The depth-gradient normal loss evidently
acts on the rendered depth, not on how the layers are made. G1 is therefore **not expected to help Spark's wall grain**.
This was not tested in Spark (G1 did not pass).
